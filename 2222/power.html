Power

H0: μ = μ0 vs H1: μ > μ0. Normal population, σ known. The truth is a specific μ*.

The page opens with the power example from class: μ0 = 5, σ = 1, n = 16, α = 0.05 and the truth μ* = 5.5. Change one thing at a time and watch the picture, the numbers on the right and the power curve.

  • Start: work out Xc, β and power for the opening values by hand, then check them against the numbers on the right.
  • Distance from μ0: drag μ* away from μ0, then back toward it. What happens to β and power?
  • At and below μ0: set μ* = μ0 = 5. What is Pr(reject H0), and why? Then move μ* below 5.
  • Significance level: click α = 0.01, then α = 0.10. Where does the cutoff move, and what happens to power?
  • Spread: make σ smaller, then make n larger. Why do both narrow the curves?

Freeze for comparison keeps a dashed grey copy of the current picture and power curve, so you can change one slider and see before and after side by side. The line under the buttons shows the old and new power and what you changed. Clear frozen removes the copy.
